On keeping a small operation steady

Hemang Champaneria

Most small operations do not fail from a lack of ideas. They strain when the ordinary work is sloppy: a promise missed, a bill unpaid, a task that only one person knows how to do.

A few habits help more than a complicated plan. Write down what was agreed, and keep the promise. Watch cash as carefully as sales; a busy week can still leave you short if money leaves faster than it arrives. Teach a second person the essential jobs before you need a substitute. Fix small problems while they are still small.

Simple systems beat clever ones. A checklist that people actually use is worth more than a process no one can remember. Review the numbers on a regular cadence, not only when something feels wrong. And when something does go wrong, look at the system before looking for someone to blame.

Steady is not dull. It is how trust is built — with the people you work with, and with the people you serve.
