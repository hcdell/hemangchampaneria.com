On making time for real work

Hemang Champaneria

It is easy to stay busy and still postpone the work that matters. Messages, meetings, and small requests can fill a day without leaving a mark. Real work — the kind that needs thought — requires a block of unbroken time and a decision to protect it.

A useful starting point is to choose one important task and finish it before opening the rest of the list. Fewer standing meetings, shorter ones, and a written purpose for each gathering give hours back. So does closing the extra tabs, and treating “quick checks” as the interruptions they are.

Learning belongs in that protected time too. It is easy to treat learning as something that ends when school ends. The more useful habit is quieter: read a little, ask a better question, and admit what you do not know yet. Most progress is slow. Patience is not a delay. It is how good work usually gets done.
